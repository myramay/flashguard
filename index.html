    Content warning for students and families

    How it works

    A flash is when part of the screen gets much brighter and then darker again (or the other way round). Flashes that are bright red are checked separately, because red is more likely to cause seizures.

    The international accessibility rule (WCAG 2.3.1) says: no more than 3 flashes in any one second, unless the flashing area is small or the change in brightness is small.

    1. FlashGuard looks at 30 pictures for every second of video.
    2. It measures how bright each area of the screen is, and how strongly red it is.
    3. It counts a flash when a quarter or more of the screen changes enough, then changes back.
    4. Any one-second stretch with more than 3 flashes is marked as a flashing part.

    This is a simplified automatic check. It is a helpful first step, not a medical or legal guarantee. If a video is important, also review it with a certified tool.

    Flashing ahead

    FlashGuard runs entirely in your browser. No video, audio or text leaves your device.
